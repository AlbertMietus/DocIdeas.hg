The costs of a mistake
======================
:Draft: Aug 2026


A typical code bug takes between one hour and one week to solve. Annoying, but the costs are minor—a
few thousand euros at most. Sure, a bug escaping your sprint is a bit more expensive. And once it
hits production, it can increase seven-fold.

But what about an architectural mistake? Let’s be honest: it is just as easy to make. Just a few
lines in a thick document. But if it’s a real architectural error, it is fundamentally wrong! Let us
take a trivial example: choosing the programming environment for a new, modern Embedded System. And
yes, those mistakes have been made.

Here is an untold story: after 300 man-years of work, it simply didn't work!
|BR|
The only solution was to adjust one single paragraph in the initial architecture; maybe an hour
work. But it implied throwing everything away, crying it out, and starting all over again.
Slightly more expensive!

What does that teach us?
|BR|
Sometimes small details are really important. For the product you work on, and for your career!  

Surely, haste is never a great driver. I would’t welcome a delay of 100% in the first sprint, when
I’m leading from the process-axis. But when I have to choose between 2 weeks and 300 man-years, it’s
an easy choice.
