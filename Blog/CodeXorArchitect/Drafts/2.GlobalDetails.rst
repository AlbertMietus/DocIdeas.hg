Global Details
==============
:Starting: Aug, 17 2026

Any senior (embedded) software engineer knows (s)he should focus on details, as the devil is in
those details. But what is a detail?
|BR|
Often, a client, as a user, will focus on the most important pieces and call everything else a
detail: *“We hardly use that”*. But still assumes every part is bug-free.

Something equivalent can be seen when we look at software from contrasting abstraction levels. To
create maintainable, bug-free code, almost every line is a detail that should get the full attention
of the software engineering team. But some details are globally important and should be supervised
by the (software) architect.
|BR|
It's hard, however, to determine which details are globally important.

Therefore, a simple analogy: a simple house.
|BR|
Typically, it has a kitchen at the base level, a toilet on every floor, and a bathroom on the second
floor. Four details that have a commonality: water and a drain. Therefore, the “house architect”
will align them vertically. Why? Simply, it reduces costs and makes building (“implementing”) a lot
easier when there is one “column” (or corner) for all those pipes.

Software is different, or not?  Ever used an API where every call had another concept? Did you *like*
it, or would it make your life a lot easier when somebody aligned those details?
|BR|
When you love to mature into a great architect, please find those details and make it easy for your team.
